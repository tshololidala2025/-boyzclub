SAFEGUARDING

Their future deserves our responsibility.

We Are Boys is designed around clear boundaries, responsible mentor screening, guardian involvement and controlled access to information about children.

Guardian involvement

Registrations for boys are completed by a parent, legal guardian or authorised caregiver. Guardians should be informed about the programme structure, expectations and any mentorship arrangements.

Mentor screening

Submitting a mentor application does not mean automatic approval. Mentor applicants should be reviewed for suitability before being matched with a child. Appropriate identity, reference and background checks should form part of the formal process before launch.

Clear mentor boundaries

Mentors should follow written rules covering communication, meetings, transport, gifts, social media, confidentiality, physical contact and reporting concerns. One-to-one contact should happen only within approved programme arrangements.

Information protection

Information about children should never be publicly displayed. Only authorised people who need the information for programme administration or safeguarding should have access to it.

Reporting concerns

We Are Boys should have a named safeguarding lead and a clear process for receiving, documenting and escalating concerns. Where a child may be at risk, the programme should follow applicable South African child-protection requirements and involve the appropriate responsible adults or authorities.

Before full launch

The safeguarding framework, mentor code of conduct, screening process, incident-reporting procedure and consent documents should receive appropriate professional review before large-scale matching begins.
